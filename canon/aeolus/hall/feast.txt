Twelve children, six of each, married to each other, which is
not how it is done where you come from and is nobody's business
but theirs.

They eat every night at the same table. The meat never runs out.
Nobody on this island has ever had to leave it to get anything.

You have been at this table for a month. Your men are fed and
rested and have stopped flinching at noises, and you have told
the story of the horse eleven times and it is better every time.

You could stay. It is not offered and you do not ask.

There is a thing about a house like this, where nothing is
lacking and nothing changes. After thirty days you notice that
none of the twelve has ever asked you a question about anywhere
except Troy, and that Troy is over.

On the last morning he walks you down to the shore himself, and
gives you a present, and nobody has ever been given a better
