Nine days.

You hold the steering oar for all of them. Not because nobody
else can -- Eurylochus can, half the older men could do it
asleep -- but because the bag is by your foot and the cord is
under your hand and you have decided, without saying it to
anyone, that this is a thing you do yourself.

You do not sleep. Nine days.

On the tenth morning there is a smudge on the water that
resolves into a coast, and then into a coast you know, and then
into your own island. Ithaca. Close enough to see the fires on
the beach where men are burning off the scrub, the way they do
in that month, the way your father did.

Close enough to make out individual men.

And that is when it goes. Nine days of not sleeping arrives all
at once and takes you down where you sit, with your hand still
on the cord.


Here is what your men have been discussing for nine days.

Aeolus gave you a present. They watched him give it to you. They
watched you put it by your foot and keep it there, awake, for
nine days, and not once say what was in it.

They have decided it is gold. Silver and gold, a parting gift
from a king, and their captain has been sitting on it and
sharing nothing, the way he came home from Troy with the best of
everything and they came home with what was left.

They are not wrong about the last part. That is what makes it
happen.
