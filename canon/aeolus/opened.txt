They untie the cord.

You are asleep and there is nothing between the bag and them but
a knot, and one of them has it undone before anyone can decide
to stop him.

Everything in there comes out at once. Not one wind -- all of
them, together, in a single instant, seven winds that have been
tied up in the dark for ten days and know exactly what they want
to do.

That is what happens when you let go of everything you were
holding, all at the same time, because you did not stop to think
about which parts you meant:

    mv aeolus/bag/*.wind aeolus/winds

Look at what just left your ship.

The sea goes white. The mast goes over. You wake up in a
horizontal world with Ithaca gone from the water behind you and
your own crew screaming, and you lie there in the bottom of the
boat and think, quite calmly, about going over the side.

You do not. That is not a decision so much as a failure to make
one.

Ten days of hard sailing, gone, in the time it takes to untie a
knot. And it is not their fault, or it is, but the man who did
not tell them what was in the bag was you.

They take you back to the floating island. The wind sees to it.
