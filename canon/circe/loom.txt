There are wolves and lions in the clearing.

Mountain wolves. Full-grown lions. They come at your men across
the open ground, every man there braces to die, but the animals
do not attack.

They fawn. They stand up against them like dogs against a man
coming home. They push their heads under their hands. They are
so plainly, desperately glad to see somebody that your men are
more frightened than they were when they thought they were being
charged.

Nobody says the obvious thing out loud. They have all thought
it.

From inside the house there is a woman singing. Not humming --
singing properly, at work, the way women sing at a loom. It is a
big loom by the sound of the shuttle. She is good.

They call up to her. She comes out at once, opens the door wide,
asks them in. She is delighted. There is nothing wrong with her
at all.

All five go in.

Eurylochus stops in the doorway, steps back off the step, cannot
tell them why, and stands outside in a clearing full of lions
instead.

He is not brave. He has never once been brave. He is the man who
argues in front of the crew and is right about as often as you
are, and this afternoon he is right again.
