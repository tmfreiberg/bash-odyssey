Eurylochus comes down that track at a dead run, gets to the
ship, and cannot speak.

Not will not -- cannot. He stands in front of you with his mouth
working and nothing coming out. It takes him a long time. When
it does come it is not organised.

Then he says: do not go up there. Push this ship off the beach
now, tonight, with the men we have.

He is your second in command, right about as often as you are,
and this afternoon he has already been right once.

You go anyway. Sword, bow, up the track alone. They are your
men. You put five of them in a helmet and shook it.


Halfway up, there is a young man on the path.

He is about the age where a man's beard is new. He is the
best-looking person you have ever seen. He takes your hand like
he knows you, then tells you exactly what is in that house and
exactly what is going to happen in it.

She will mix a cup and put a drug in it. The drug will not work
on you. When it does not work she will come at you with the rod.
You are to draw your sword and go at her hard, as though you
mean to kill her. She will be frightened. Then she will invite
you to her bed, and you are not to refuse -- but before anything
else, she swears an oath.

Then he gives you something.

He does not hand it over in the open. Gods do not put things in
your hand where anyone can see. There is a way of looking at a
place that shows you what a plain look does not:

    ls -a circe

That was true in the cave. It is true on this path. It will be
true on every floor you ever stand on.

When you look up he is gone, in the way they go, and you are
standing on a track in an oak wood holding a plant.

You know who that was. The young one, the messenger, the gold
rod, the sandals. Hermes. He has never once come to you before
in twenty years of asking.
