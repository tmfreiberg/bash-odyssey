Aiaia. Nobody aboard knows whether it lies east or west of
anywhere. Neither do you, and you have been navigating since you
were fourteen.

You lie on that beach for two days and a night with your face in
your arms and you do not speak to anyone. Neither does anybody
else. There were four hundred men in a harbour a week ago. Now
there is one hull pulled up on sand, and everybody on it has
somebody he is not talking about.

On the third day you go up to high ground, and there is smoke.
One column, inland, out of a thick oak wood. Nothing else in any
direction but sea.

On the way back down you kill a stag. A big one, coming to the
river to drink. You carry it back across your shoulders with its
legs tied, and it is heavy enough that you have to stop twice.

They eat. It is the first thing that has gone right since
Aeolus. By dark they are talking again. Somebody laughs at
something. You sit outside the firelight, listening, and feel
almost level.

Then you tell them about the smoke, and it stops.

They have been in a cave and a harbour. They know what inland
means now. But there is no water on this beach and you cannot
sit here. It has to be done, and the argument is only about who
does it.
