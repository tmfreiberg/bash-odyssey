A year.

That is the part nobody tells properly. Not a night, not a
season. Twelve months in a stone house in an oak wood, with meat
and wine every day and nothing whatever to do. It goes past the
way that kind of time goes past: all at once, with nothing to
hang it on.

You are not held. There is no drug in it, no spell. The door is
open every single day of that year.

The men mend. They put on weight. They stop waking in the night.

You write nothing. There is a log on that ship and it does not
get a line all year. Afterwards, when you try to reckon up where
the ten years went, this is the part you cannot account for.
This is the part your wife will ask about.

At the end of it one of them comes to you. Not Eurylochus -- one
of the ordinary ones, and he says it carefully, the way a man
speaks to someone he is not sure of any more:

It is time. If we are ever going home, it is time.

You realise you have not thought about Ithaca in months.
Nothing was done to you to bring that about. This is simply what
it is like. It is not the lotus. Nobody made you.

You go to her that night and ask to be let go.
