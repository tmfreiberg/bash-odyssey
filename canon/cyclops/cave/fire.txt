EITHER WAY, AT SOME POINT IN THE NIGHT, THE STAKE GOES IN.

He screams. Not like a man. He tears it out and throws it and
then he starts shouting up the hillside for his neighbours, and
they come as far as the rock and ask him through it who is
hurting him.

And he tells them, truthfully, that Nobody is.

They tell him that if no one is attacking him then this is a
sickness sent by Zeus, and there is nothing to be done about it,
and he should pray to his father Poseidon. Then they go back to
bed.

There is a thing happening in his language that cannot happen in
yours. The word he is shouting and the word for cunning are the
same letters. Only the spacing differs, and the way the voice
goes up. Nobody is killing me, he is howling into the dark, all
night, and what he is also saying, exactly, is: cunning is
killing me.

You lie under a sheep in the corner of the cave and listen to a
blind giant tell the truth about you in a language you barely
speak, and your heart laughs.

In the morning he is blind and the door is open, because a blind
man still has to let his sheep out.

He is standing in the doorway with his hands out, feeling every
