He looks at you the way you look at something caught in a net.

He asks who you are and where you are from, and there is no
politeness in it. He is asking the way a man asks a sheep which
field it wandered out of.

Give him a name that is no use to him. Give him Outis.

It is the Greek for "no one," and it is also close enough to a
real name that he will not think twice about it -- the sort of
thin, unlucky name a poor man from a poor island might actually
be stuck with. Which is what you look like, standing in his
doorway with nothing.

This matters more than it sounds. If anything happens to him in
the night he will shout for help, and what he shouts is the name
you gave him, and the other Cyclopes on this hillside are as
large as he is.

So your name cannot be on the ship tonight and it cannot be
Odysseus. It has to be in this cave, and it has to be Outis.

The file is ship/crew/odysseus.txt. Where it has to end up is
cyclops/cave/outis.txt. That is one file moved and renamed in
the same breath, not a copy left behind on the deck, and it is
one command:

    less scrolls/mv.txt

If you would rather he knew who you were, leave it where it is.
Some men would.

He listens to the name, and thinks about it, and says that he
will eat Outis last. He means it as a guest-gift. It is the only
joke he makes.

Then he eats two of your men. Not a fight, not a struggle -- he
picks them up and that is the end of them. Then he lies down
across the door and sleeps.

Two men who walked in here with you are not walking out. Choose
which two from ship/crew and rm them. You knew these ones. You
will have to say their names to their families, if you ever see
their families.

It is the same command it was at Ismarus. It does not get easier
and it should not.

Tonight you sleep in a room with a door you cannot open, and you
do not sleep much, and some time before morning you get up and
walk the length of the cave looking at what he keeps in it:

    less cyclops/cave/pens.txt
