The big ram is always first out of the cave. Every morning of
his life, at the front, with the flock behind him.

This morning he is last, because you are underneath him with
both hands in his wool and he is carrying a man's weight he has
never carried before.

The giant stops him in the doorway. He is fond of this one. He
puts his hands on the ram's back, an inch above your face, and
asks him why he is last today when he is always first. Asks
whether he is grieving. Asks whether he is sorry about the eye,
and about the thing called Nobody who did it.

Then he lets him through.

Do not stop to count anything until the oars are in the water.
