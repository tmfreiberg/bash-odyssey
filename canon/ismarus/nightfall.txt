You should have gone at dawn. You said so twice and then you
stood on the beach and let it happen, and the thing about being
in command is that there is nobody else to blame for the hour
you did not insist.

They came out of the hills with the sun behind them, thick as
leaves. There were more of them than you and they were better
at it than you, and they had chariots.

You held the line all morning. When the sun went down you did
not hold it any more.

Six men from every ship. On your own ship that means six of the
crew, and you are the one who has to say which.

There are twenty men in ship/crew and you have known six of
them for ten years, and one of those six is you. The rest are
strangers. You will know them when you read them: a detail each,
and none of it anything you went out of your way to learn.

Six of the strangers are dead. Which six is yours to say.

Read them and choose the six. Then take them off the roster,
because a dead man is not crew and the file should not be there
any more:

    less scrolls/rm.txt

There is no bin it goes to. There is no way to get them back.
That is not a limitation of the tool. That is what the tool is
for.

Then go and look at what the day bought you:

    ls ship/hold

Then write it down. Ten years of this and you have started to
lose the order of it -- what happened where, which island was
which, who you were when you did it. From tonight you keep a
log, and you keep it up, and at the end of all this it will be
the only thing you have that nobody can take:

    less scrolls/cat.txt
    less scrolls/redirection.txt

The log is at ship/log.txt. There is already something in it.
Add to it -- do not write over it -- and that distinction is the
whole of the second scroll.

Nine days of open water after that, with the wind off Cape Malea
blowing the wrong way the whole time. On the tenth there is
land, and fresh water, and nobody on the beach to meet you.

    less lotus/shore.txt
