The cave above the beach belongs to the nymphs. There are stone
mixing bowls in it, stone jars, stone looms where they weave sea
purple. Two entrances: one for men, one that only gods use.

The Phaeacians stacked your gifts here while you slept:

Everything you own in the world, in a cave, on a beach, three
miles from your own house.

Athena helps you carry it deeper in. Then she puts a stone
across the mouth herself.

Then she sits down on the roots of the olive tree to tell you
what is in your house.

A hundred and eight men. Not soldiers: the sons of the best
families on Ithaca, Same, Dulichium, Zacynthus. They have been
living in your hall for three years, eating your herds, emptying
your cellar, giving orders to your household, waiting for your
wife to give up on you.

Your son is twenty. He sailed to Pylos looking for news of you;
they have a ship waiting in the straits to kill him on the way
home.

Your wife has held them off for three years with a loom. She
told them she would choose when the shroud for your father was
woven. She unwove it every night by torchlight. They found out
last year.

Your father has left the town. He is in the orchard, sleeping
with the farm hands in winter, in the leaves in summer.

You ask her how one man walks into a hall with a hundred and
eight men in it.

She says: not as yourself.
