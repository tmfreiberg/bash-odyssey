There is a last fight. It is over quickly.

The families of the dead men come out to the orchard with
weapons. Laertes, who is seventy-something, throws first, kills
the father of the man you shot through the throat with your
second arrow. Then Athena shouts once, in her own voice, from
the middle of the field.

Everybody drops everything they are holding.

Zeus puts a bolt into the ground in front of her to make the
point. She tells them to stop. They stop. She makes an oath
between them that holds afterwards, so that the island does not
spend the next thirty years killing itself over a hall full of
suitors.

That is the end of the story. It goes out on a truce in an
orchard rather than on anything triumphant.


You are home. Twenty years.

Nobody who sailed with you is. Every man on the roster at
Ismarus is on the bottom of the sea somewhere between here and
Troy, or sitting in a grove full of flowers, or eaten in a cave
by something that could not see him.

Read your log. All of it, in one go, from Troy:

    less ship/log.txt

That is the record you kept by hand, on nights when you were
tired, in your own words, on a ship that no longer exists.

    wc -l ship/log.txt

Count how many lines ten years came to.


Now the last command of this voyage.

There is a version of your story that never changed -- the one
this game was dealt from, sitting in canon/, untouched since the
morning you first ran begin.sh. Every choice you made since
Ismarus made your copy diverge from it.

Set them against each other:

    diff -r canon play

You rehearsed this in a meadow on Thrinacia, on a herd of cattle
you had copied the night before, which was one command that cost
you nothing at the time.

Read what comes back.

Every "Only in canon" is something you destroyed: a man you
took off the roster, a fleet you deleted in a harbour, a hold
that went down with a ship.

Every "Only in play" is something you made: a log, an oath, a
raft, a trench, a name in a cave, a son.

Every "differ" is a file you touched.

That listing is the difference between the Odyssey that was
lying on this disk when you started, the one you actually had.

Nobody else's looks like it.


    alias

One more thing, since you are home, since you own this house.

    less scrolls/alias.txt

Make yourself a command. Any command, in whatever shape fits
your hand. The man of many turns, of many devices, of many
names, ends his voyage by naming a tool after himself.

Then, if you want to sail again, there is a fresh copy waiting
in canon/, dealt out by one line of shell you now understand
completely:

    ./begin.sh --restart
