Your wife brings the bow down herself.

It belonged to Iphitus, who gave it to you when you were both
young men, before somebody murdered him. You never took it to
Troy. It has been in the store room for twenty years because it
was too good to risk.

She sets the terms: twelve axe heads in a line, the way you used
to do it. Whoever strings this bow, sends an arrow clean through
all twelve, takes me out of this house.

They try in order. They warm the horn at the fire, they grease
it, they brace it against the floor. Not one of them gets the
string near the notch. Antinous says they should leave it for
tomorrow, which is a way of saying he cannot do it in front of
people.

The beggar in the corner asks for a turn.

The hall finds this very funny for about as long as it takes to
put the bow into his hands.

The bow is a program rather than a page. Run it:

    bash ithaca/hall/bow.sh

It looks for you. Not for a beggar, not for a stranger from
Crete -- for a name, anywhere on this island, in whatever place
you last put it. If you have kept your name somewhere findable,
the horn comes round.

If nothing on this island belongs to you any more, it will tell
you so, then tell you how to look.
