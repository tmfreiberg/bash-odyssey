Eumaeus keeps your pigs. He was born a king's son on an island
called Syrie, stolen by traders at about five years old, bought
by your father, raised in your house alongside your sister.

He has built himself a stockade out of quarried stone with a
thorn hedge on top, twelve sties inside it, because nobody told
him to let the place go while the master was away.

His dogs come at you like wolves, four of them, silent, which is
worse than barking. You sit down in the dirt with your stick
out of your hand, because a man who stands still on the ground
is not worth biting.

Eumaeus comes out of the shed at a run, shouting, throwing
stones until they scatter. He says: old man, another second and
they would have had you. That would have been mine to carry.

Then he takes you inside, spreads brushwood, throws his own
goat-skin over it for you to sit on. He kills two young pigs.
He gives you the long cut off the back. That is the guest's
portion, off a man who has nothing spare.

He talks about his master the whole evening. His master is dead,
he says; some liar comes through here every month with a story
about him, they tell it for a bed, he feeds them anyway.

You tell him a lie. It is a long one, Cretan again, better than
it needs to be. He listens the way a man listens to something he
has stopped believing.

Then he says the thing you will remember:

Old man, do not try to please me with news of him. I loved him.
He is gone. I say his name out loud when I am on my own with the
pigs.

He has no idea. He will not know for four more days.
