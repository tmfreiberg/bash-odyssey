You go out to the orchard in the morning.

Your father is on his knees in the dirt with a spade, banking up
a vine. Patched tunic. Stitched leather gaiters against the
brambles. Goatskin cap. He is dressed for nobody, because nobody
comes out here.

He is very old.

You stand under a pear tree watching him work for a while, then
you do the thing you have done on every island of this voyage.
You tell him a lie.

You ask him whose garden this is, you say you are looking for a
man you entertained years ago, you say the name, you watch your
own father put dirt on his head with both hands over a son you
say has been missing for twenty years.

It takes you eleven more lines to stop.

Then: I am he. I am here. I am home.

He wants proof. Everybody wants proof. You give him the scar,
then you give him the better one, because he taught you to know
a tree the way other men know their neighbours:

You were a boy following him around this orchard asking for
things, so he gave them to you, one at a time, walking:
thirteen pear trees, ten apple, forty fig, fifty rows of vines
ripening at different times so there would be grapes all season.

You say the numbers back to him.

That is what finishes it. Not the scar: the inventory. A boy who
was given a list of trees by his father in the year he was old
enough to remember it, saying it back nearly forty years on, in
the same orchard, correct.

His knees go. You catch him.
