There was an olive tree with long leaves growing inside the
walls of the yard.

He built the chamber around it, out of close-set stone, roofed
it, put doors on it.

He cut the crown off the olive, trimmed the trunk from the root
up, smoothed it with an adze, worked it straight to a line,
drilled it through with an auger.

He made it the bedpost. He worked the frame out from there,
inlaid it with gold, with silver, with ivory. He strung it with
ox-hide thongs dyed sea purple.

One leg of that bed is a root still in the ground.

Nobody has ever moved it. It cannot be moved by any living man
unless he takes a saw to the post.

Nobody knows this but the two of us, one servant, the tree.
