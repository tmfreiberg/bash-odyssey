The shepherd asks what country you think this is.

You tell him a lie. A good one: long, detailed, a murdered man
in it, a voyage out of Crete, a plausible reason for the
treasure stacked on the sand. Twenty years of practice,
delivered without a pause, to a boy on a beach.

The boy listens to all of it, smiling.

Then the boy stops being a boy. She stands there in her own
shape, tall, grey-eyed, looking at you the way you look at
something you are fond of that has just done something
predictable.

She says: it would take a sharp man to get past you. Even a god.
Even knowing who you are. You are the best of all men at
planning, at talking; I am the best of all the gods at both. You
did not know me. That is the only reason you are still lying to
me.

Athena. She was on a beach at Ismarus. She was in a cave under a
dot in the filename. She has been at your shoulder for twenty
years without once putting her hand on the tiller for you.

She tells you where you are. She lifts the mist.

It is the harbour of Phorcys, the old sea god: two headlands
folding in on it so that a ship rides inside without a mooring
rope. At the head of the beach there is an olive with long
leaves, older than anybody, the tree every fisherman on this
coast gives directions by. Beside it, back in the rock, a cave
where the nymphs are given offerings. Behind all of it, Neriton,
the wooded mountain.

You know each of those the way you know your own hands. You have
known them since before you could walk.

Ithaca.

You put your face in the ground. You kiss the dirt of it.
