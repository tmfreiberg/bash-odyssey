Eleven ships along the wall, tied bow to stern, close enough
that a man can walk the length of the fleet without touching
water.

They are all in there. Everything you have except the hull under
your own feet.

    ls -R laestrygonia/harbour

That is one listing of the whole harbour: every ship, and what
is aboard each of them. If -R is new to you, it is in the same
scroll it always was:

    less scrolls/ls.txt

Read a few. Not all eleven -- you have not got the time, and you
would not do it anyway, because you have had ten years to do it
and you never have:

    less laestrygonia/harbour/ship_07/crew.txt

Every one of those files starts with a number, and the number is
men.

You do not know what they come to. You have never known. Eleven
ships is a thing you say, and it has never once been a quantity
of people in your head, and tonight is the last night it would
have been possible to find out.

So find out. There is a way of pulling one line out of every
file in a directory and putting the whole lot in front of you at
once, and it is the search command you already have:

    grep -h men laestrygonia/harbour/*/crew.txt

Wildcards, from the last island. grep, from the one before. The
-h is so it prints the lines without the filenames in front of
them.

Then add them up yourself, on your fingers, the way anyone would.
It comes to something over four hundred.

They are asleep on that wall. Almost all of them are from your
islands and about a hundred are from Ithaca itself, and their
mothers know your name.

Now send three men up the track to find out whose harbour this
is, because that is what you always do and it has never once
gone well:

    less laestrygonia/well.txt
