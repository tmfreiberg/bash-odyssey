They throw rocks.

Not thrown the way a man throws. Each one is more than a man
could lift, and there are thousands of them along the top on
both sides, and the harbour below is a hundred yards of flat
water with eleven ships in it and no way out except a gap two
ships wide.

The noise is the thing your men will not talk about afterwards.
Timber going, and men in the water, and then the men in the
water going quiet in a particular order as the rocks work down
the line.

Then they come down with fishing spears and take them out of the
water one at a time, like fish, because that is what it is. It
is not a battle. Nobody up there is in any danger. They are
getting their supper and they will be at it until dark.

Every ship in that harbour is gone. Not damaged -- gone, and
their crews with them, and there is not one thing you can do
about any of it from where you are standing.

Look at it first. Do the recursive listing one more time and
read the names of what is in there, because after the next
command there is no list of it anywhere:

    ls -R laestrygonia/harbour

Then take the harbour off the world.

    rm -r laestrygonia/harbour

Count what it cost you before you row. You did the arithmetic an
hour ago, on the deck, with a pipe and a listing, and the number
came to four hundred and sixty men.

You will not be able to do it again. The listing is gone and so
are they, and the only place that number exists now is in your
own log if you put it there.
