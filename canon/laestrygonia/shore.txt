Six days rowing, with no wind at all, which is either what
Aeolus arranged or what the sea does to a man it has stopped
being interested in.

On the seventh there is a coast, and a gap in it.

The harbour is behind a mouth so narrow that two ships cannot
pass in it, and the cliffs on either side go straight up out of
the water, and inside there is no swell at all. Not a ripple. A
sailor who has been at sea for six days looks at water like that
the way a thirsty man looks at a cup.

Eleven ships go in and tie up along the wall.

You keep yours outside. Made fast to a rock at the harbour
mouth, on the open sea, with the swell under her.

You could not tell anybody why. There is no reason and there was
no argument and nobody asked you for one. It is the same
instinct that made you wait in the Cyclops' cave, and that one
was wrong, and this one is about to be the only reason there is
anybody left to remember either.

Go and look at the fleet while it is still there:

    less laestrygonia/anchorage.txt

This island ends on the water, the way the last one did:

    laestrygonia/open_water.txt

Read that when there is nothing else left, and not before.
