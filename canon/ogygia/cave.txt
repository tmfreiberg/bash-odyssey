The cave is in a rock face with woodland grown up around the
mouth: alder, black poplar, cypress. Long-winged birds roost in
it -- owls, hawks, sea crows that work the water all day.

A vine runs across the entrance, heavy with grapes, cultivated
by somebody.

Four springs rise within a few yards of each other, then run off
in four directions. Meadows of violet on both sides, soft
ground, wet enough to stay green.

Homer says it plainly: a god arriving here would stop to look at
it before going in.

Inside there is cedar burning, split, scenting the whole
headland. She works at a golden loom with a golden shuttle,
singing. She is always singing. In seven years you never once
hear her sing the same thing twice.

There is no lack of anything on this island. No hunger, no
weather to speak of, nothing that needs doing, nobody to answer
to.

You spend your days on a rock above the beach looking at water.
