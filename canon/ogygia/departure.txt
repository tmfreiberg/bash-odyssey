On the fifth day she washes you, dresses you, puts bread aboard,
a skin of water, a second skin of dark wine, provisions enough
to matter. Then she sends a following wind, warm, steady, going
the right way.

You are glad of the wind. You spread the sail to it.

She stands on the beach. Neither of you says anything that
belongs in a poem.

Seventeen days.

You do not sleep for any of them. You steer by the Pleiades, by
Bootes going down late, by the Bear -- the one that turns in
place watching Orion, the only one that never bathes in the
Ocean. She told you to keep it on your left hand the whole way.
You do.

Seventeen days of open water on twenty pegged logs, alone,
sailing a course a goddess gave you off the top of her head,
navigating by a star you have known since you were a boy on a
different island.

On the eighteenth day there are mountains on the horizon. Low,
dark, close, like a shield laid on the water. Land with people
on it.

Then Poseidon, coming back from Ethiopia, looks down from the
mountains of the Solymi.

He has not seen you since a cave on a hillside above a goat
island, where a blind man told him a name. He sees you now, on a
raft, eighteen days out, three hours from a coast.

He says, out loud, to nobody: the gods have changed their minds
about this man while I was away. He is very close to shore. I do
not think he is there yet.

Write it down before it starts. This is the last dry moment you
