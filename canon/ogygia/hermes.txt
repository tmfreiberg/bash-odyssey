In the eighth year somebody argues about you.

Not here. On Olympus, at a meeting you know nothing about, while
you are sitting on your rock. Athena raises it: he is on an
island, he has been there seven years, the woman keeping him has
no intention of stopping, meanwhile his house is full of men
eating his cattle.

Zeus sends Hermes.

You have met him once, on a track in an oak wood, the day he
pulled a black root out of hard ground for you. He does not come
to you this time. He goes straight past you to her.

He does not enjoy the errand. He says so out loud when he lands:
it is a long way across empty salt water, there is not one city
on the route, nobody sacrificing to anybody. He is a god
complaining about a commute.

Then he tells her: Zeus says let him go.

Her reply is the best speech any goddess makes in the whole
poem, and it is not about you.

She says: you are cruel, all of you, jealous past bearing. You
cannot stand it when one of us takes a mortal man to her bed.
You never could. Then she lists them -- Orion, hunted down by
Artemis. Iasion, killed by a thunderbolt in a ploughed field.
Every time, one of you puts a stop to it.

Then she says the thing that stays with you afterwards: I saved
him. He came here on a keel, alone, everyone else dead. I fed
him. I loved him. I was going to make him deathless.

Then, because she is a goddess, because Zeus is Zeus: fine. Let
him go. I have no ship. I have no crew. I will give him what
advice I can.

She keeps every word of that.
