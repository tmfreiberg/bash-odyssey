She makes you an offer. She makes it more than once, over years,
in different words.

Stay here. Not stay a while: stay. She will make you deathless,
ageless, permanently. Not a long life -- no life at all in the
sense you understand, because nothing would be counting down.

Consider what is on the table. You have watched six men go up a
cliff face calling your name. You have watched a ship full of
men go into the water in one second. You have stood in a trench
of blood on a black beach holding a sword between your own
mother and the thing she needed. Every one of those happened
because men die.

She is offering the removal of the entire category.

Set against it: an island the size of a good farm, a woman
twenty years older than the last time you saw her, a son who
was a baby, a father sleeping in the ashes by the fire in
winter.

Your answer, which you give her more than once, in the same
words each time:

I know. My wife is not your equal in form or in stature. She is
mortal, you are deathless, ageless. I know that. All the same,
every day I want to go home. If a god wrecks me again on the
open sea I will bear it -- I have a heart in me by now that is
used to bearing things.

That is the whole argument you ever make. She does not accept
it. She does not have to: there is no boat on this island, no
crew, no timber cut, no way off.
