She gives you a bronze axe, double-bladed, fitted to an olive
handle. An adze. Then she walks you to the far end of the island
where the timber is.

Alder, poplar, silver fir, standing dead a long time, seasoned,
dry, light in the water.

You cut twenty trees.

Twenty. Trimmed, squared with the adze, bored through with the
augers she brings you, pegged to each other, fitted edge to edge
like the deck of a wide merchant ship. Rails. A mast. A
steering oar. Woven willow along the sides against the sea.
Cloth for a sail, which she cuts herself.

Four days. Not one man has ever built a boat faster.

This is the first thing you have made with your hands since you
came home from Troy. Everything else in ten years has been
taking, losing, escaping, burying.

There is no raft in this world yet. Make one:

    mkdir ogygia/raft

Then the planks. Twenty of them, numbered, all at once -- you do
not fell twenty trees one at a time by name any more than you
would type twenty filenames:

    less scrolls/braces.txt

    touch ogygia/raft/plank_{01..20}.txt

Then the rest of her:

    touch ogygia/raft/{mast,sail,rudder,rail}.txt

Count what you have built:

    ls ogygia/raft | wc -l

Twenty-four pieces. It is not a ship. Twelve ships left Troy
behind you; this is twenty logs pegged together by one man with
a borrowed axe, which is what is going to carry you the rest of
the way home.

    less ogygia/departure.txt
