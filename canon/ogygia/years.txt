Seven years.

Not a season, not a stretch. Seven times through the whole
turning of the year on an island where the year does not turn
much. Longer than the war. Longer than everything between Troy
and this beach put together, twice over.

Nothing happens in them. That is not an omission in the telling.
Homer gives seven years the space he gives an afternoon, because
there is nothing in them to give space to.

The days go: the rock above the beach, the water, the horizon
with nothing on it. Weeping, at first. Then not weeping, which
is worse, because it means the thing has settled.

At night you go back to the cave. She is willing; you are not,
much; the arrangement holds anyway, in the way arrangements do
when one party owns the island.

You write nothing. There is no log here. There is no ship to
keep it on.

So the only record of your life on this voyage is the one you
made without meaning to.

    less scrolls/history.txt

Every command you have typed, in order, numbered, kept by the
machine while you were busy:

    history

Too much of it to read. Cut it the way you cut the Sirens'
song:

    history | wc -l

Then look at what you actually did. Not what happened to you --
what you did, with your hands:

    history | grep rm

There it is. Six at Ismarus, on a beach, in the dark, when they
were still strangers to you. Two in a cave. One man on a track
above a harbour, because a girl at a well was helpful. Elpenor
on Circe's island, if he was still aboard to fall off anything
-- some of that boy's luck was decided in a grove full of
flowers a year and a half earlier. Six against a cliff face
while you watched a whirlpool. A whole crew, all at once, in
open water.

Every one of those is a line you typed. Nobody typed it for you.

    history | grep "ship/crew"

That is the roster, from twenty men down to one, in your own
keystrokes.

One thing worth knowing before you rely on it: that record
belongs to the window you are sitting in. Close it, come back
tomorrow, some of the earlier voyage may be gone. Which is
correct, on this island. Nobody keeps the whole of it. You are
already losing pieces, sitting on a rock in the sun with nothing
to write on.

    less ogygia/hermes.txt
