Alcinous speaks first. He says: nobody is sending this man away
with nothing.

Twelve lords hold rank on this island, thirteen counting the
king. Each one brings a cloak, a tunic, a talent of gold. The
king adds a cup. Arete has a chest packed, ties the lid herself
with a knot she teaches you, because she is a queen who has
thought about theft.

They carry it aboard their own ship while you sleep, stow it
under the benches where nothing will shift in a swell.

Their ship. Not yours. You have not had a ship since a
thunderbolt went into one south of Thrinacia; you came off that
water on a keel, you crossed to this island on twenty logs you
cut yourself, those logs are in pieces two days out from this
beach. What you own is a log file, some clothes a girl gave you
at a river mouth, this.

It is worth more than everything you took out of Troy.

Sit with that a moment. Ten years to take one city. Twelve ships
loaded with what came out of it, every one of them riding low
when they left the beach.

None of that plunder exists any more. It is on the bottom in
four or five places between this island and Troy, most of it
still stowed under the benches of ships that went down with
their crews aboard.

You are going home richer than you ever were, carrying goods
given freely by people you had never heard of, who asked you for
nothing, who wanted no explanation beyond the story.

There is nowhere aboard your own vessel to put any of it,
because you have no vessel. Stack it where it is, on this
island, ready to go over the side at the other end:

    mkdir phaeacia/gifts
    touch phaeacia/gifts/{cloak,tunic,gold,cup,chest}.txt

    ls phaeacia/gifts

Five things. Every one from somebody who owed you nothing.

Look at where your hold used to be, while you are here:

    ls ship

Bronze, cloth, grain, a jar from a priest of Apollo -- all of it
went into eighty fathoms with the men who loaded it. You are
going home with a stranger's cloak on your back, carrying gifts
from a country whose name your own people will not recognise.

    less phaeacia/departure.txt
