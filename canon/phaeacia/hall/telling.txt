So you tell them.

You start with the name. Out loud, unprompted, to a hall full of
strangers -- the thing you did in every port for twenty years,
the thing you did once in a cave, which cost you every man who
sailed with you.

I am Odysseus, son of Laertes. Men know me for my tricks. My
fame goes up to heaven.

    cat ship/crew/odysseus.txt

That file has been on your roster since Ismarus. If it is not
there, it is in a cave on a hillside under another name, which
is a thing you will have to explain to this room.

Then the rest of it. Everything since Troy, in order, in one
sitting, to people who have never lost anybody.


Here is the difficulty. Ten years is not in one place. It is
scattered over this whole voyage in pieces you wrote on
different nights, on different islands, for different reasons.

Find them:

    find . -name "*.txt" -newer start_here.txt

Or name them, since you know what they are: your log, the
prophecy a dead man gave you, the oaths, whatever you poured
into a trench at the edge of the world.

cat takes as many files as you give it, in the order you give
them, joining them into one stream. That is what the word means.
It has meant that since Ismarus; this is the first time you have
needed it:

    cat ship/log.txt underworld/prophecy.txt

Now: you are speaking this aloud to a hall. Demodocus is sitting
three seats away with a lyre on his knee, listening to every
word, doing what bards do with what they hear. These people are
sailors. They carry things between islands. Whatever you say
tonight leaves this room whether you like it or not.

So it goes two places at once: out into the hall, down into a
record.

    less scrolls/tee.txt

    cat ship/log.txt underworld/prophecy.txt | tee phaeacia/hall/told.txt

Add the rest as you get to it, in whatever order you told it:

    cat circe/oath.txt | tee -a phaeacia/hall/told.txt
    cat ogygia/oath.txt | tee -a phaeacia/hall/told.txt
    cat thrinacia/oath_sworn.txt | tee -a phaeacia/hall/told.txt
    cat underworld/trench_offering.txt | tee -a phaeacia/hall/told.txt

Some of those files do not exist. That is not an error to fix:
it is the shape of your particular voyage. A man who never made
Circe swear anything has nothing to read out at that point in
the evening.

Then find out what ten years came to:

    wc -l phaeacia/hall/told.txt

That is the number. Every man you sailed with, every island,
every god who took an interest, in however many lines you
happened to write.

Nobody in that hall says anything for a while after you stop.

    less phaeacia/gifts.txt
