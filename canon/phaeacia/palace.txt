Bronze walls with a frieze of blue enamel. Gold doors on silver
posts, a silver lintel, a gold handle. Dogs either side of the
entrance made of gold, made of silver, made by a god, deathless,
ageless, permanent watchmen who will never need feeding.

Inside: seats along both walls with woven covers on them, gold
figures of young men holding torches to light the hall at night.
Fifty women in the household. An orchard outside where pear
follows pear, apple follows apple, fig follows fig, year round,
without a break, because the west wind here ripens one crop
while it is setting the next.

Nobody on this island wants for anything. Nobody on this island
has ever been raided.

You walk in under a mist Athena put around you, cross the whole
floor unseen, put your hands on the queen's knees before anybody
knows a stranger is in the room.

Her name is Arete. Nausicaa was right about her: her husband
consults her, the people bring her their disputes, she settles
them.

She has you given a chair. She has you fed. She does not ask who
you are for a long time, which is manners.

Then she asks where you got the clothes, because she wove them
herself.
