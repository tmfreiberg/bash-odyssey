Two days in the water after Poseidon breaks the raft. Two days
without land, without anything to hold, with a goddess's scarf
round your chest because a sea nymph came up out of the swell to
give you one.

Then a river mouth. You come in over the bar, you crawl out of
the shallows, you lie on the bank unable to move, your skin
salt-burned, your body swollen, the sea running out of your
mouth.

Above the bank there is an olive thicket, wild growth tangled
into cultivated. You go in on your hands, scrape together a
drift of dead leaves, work your way underneath.

Homer's picture for it: a man on an outlying farm with no
neighbour near enough to borrow fire from banks a live coal deep
in the ashes at nightfall, covering it over, keeping the seed of
it alive until morning.

You are the coal. There is nothing left of you above the
tideline except the part that has to last until light.

Then you sleep.

Scheria. The island of the Phaeacians, who are related to the
gods, who live a long way from everybody, who are the finest
sailors in the world.

In the morning there are girls on the beach.
