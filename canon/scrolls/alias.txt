alias -- give a command your own name

    alias look='ls -la'

From now on, in this window, typing look runs ls -la. The shell
substitutes one for the other before it does anything else.

Quote the whole definition. Use single quotes unless you have a
reason not to.

    alias home='cd ~'
    alias islands='ls /path/to/play'

To see what you have defined:

    alias

To take one away:

    unalias look

Like history, an alias belongs to the window you are sitting in.
Close it, it is gone. To keep one, put the line in the file your
shell reads when it starts -- usually ~/.bashrc -- which is a
hidden file in your home directory, findable the way you found
things in a cave a long time ago:

    ls -a ~

This is the last scroll. It is the only one that does not teach
you a tool somebody else made. It teaches you to make your own,
out of the ones you have, in whatever shape fits your hand.

Nobody else's shell has your aliases in it. That is the point of
them.
