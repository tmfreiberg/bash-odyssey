echo -- print something

    echo hello

It prints hello. That is all it does, and it is one of the most
used commands there is, because printing something is how you
get it into a file:

    echo hello > greeting.txt

Now greeting.txt contains one line. The > and >> work exactly as
they did with cat -- > destroys what was there, >> adds to the
end -- but you do not have to sit typing and press Ctrl-D. One
line, one command, done.

    echo and another line >> greeting.txt

If what you are printing has punctuation in it, put it in
quotes. The shell treats several characters as instructions
rather than as text, and quotes tell it not to:

    echo "I swear it, and I will not go back on it." > oath.txt

Use quotes whenever there is anything in the line but plain
words. It costs two characters and it removes a whole category
of surprise.
