find -- look through everything below a place

ls shows you one directory. find walks the whole tree under a
name, every level of it, reporting what it meets.

    find .

The dot means "here". That prints every file, every directory,
below where you stand. On a large tree it is far too much, which
is why find takes tests.

Match by name:

    find . -name "outis.txt"

Wildcards work, quoted so the shell hands the pattern to find
rather than expanding it first:

    find . -name "*.wind"

Match only files, or only directories:

    find . -type f
    find . -type d

Start somewhere other than here by naming the place:

    find ship/crew -type f

Combine tests. They all have to be true:

    find . -type f -name "*.txt"

find prints one result per line, which makes it the natural
front of a pipe:

    find ship/crew -type f | wc -l
    find . -name "*.wind" | sort

That is the real use of it. ls answers "what is in this room".
find answers "where in this whole house is the thing I am
thinking of, if it is anywhere".
