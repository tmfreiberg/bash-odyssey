history -- what you have typed

The shell keeps a record of your commands.

    history

Every one, numbered, oldest first. On a long session that is a
great deal of output, so send it through the tools you already
have:

    history | wc -l
    history | grep rm
    history | tail -n 20

That second one is the interesting shape. It is not a list of
what happened to you: it is a list of what you did, in order,
in your own words, kept without your asking.

Run a numbered line again by putting an exclamation mark in
front of the number:

    !412

The line before this one:

    !!

Be careful with both. The shell does not stop to consider what
it is re-running. If line 412 removed something, !412 removes
something else. Look at the line before you fire it.

Search backwards through the record without printing all of it
by pressing Ctrl-R, then typing part of the command you want.
Press Ctrl-R again to keep going further back. Enter runs what
you have found; Ctrl-G gets you out.

One limit worth knowing. This record belongs to the shell you
are sitting in. Close the window, open a new one, some of what
you did may not be there any more, depending on how your
machine is set up. The past is only as long as the session.
