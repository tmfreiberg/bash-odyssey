ls -- list

Shows what is in the place you are standing.

    ls

Names with a slash after them, or in a different colour, are
directories: places you can go. The rest are files: things you
can read.

To look somewhere without going there, name it:

    ls town

And to see more than the names -- how big a thing is, when it
was last touched:

    ls -l

The -l is an option. Most commands take options, and they
usually start with a dash.
To look inside a directory, and inside every directory in it,
all the way down:

    ls -R town

That is one listing of everything underneath a name. On a small
directory it is a convenience. On a large one it is the only way
to know what you are actually looking at.
