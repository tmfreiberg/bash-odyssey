Pipes -- | -- and wc

You know that > sends what a command prints into a file. A pipe
sends what a command prints into another command:

    ls | wc -l

Read it left to right. ls lists the files. Instead of the list
going to the screen, it goes into wc, which counts things. The
-l means count lines. One file per line, so: how many files.

wc on its own counts lines, words and characters in a file:

    wc ship/log.txt

and with -l, -w or -c you ask for just one of the three:

    wc -l ship/log.txt

Pipes work with anything that prints and anything that reads.

    grep wine ship/log.txt | wc -l

That is: find every line mentioning wine, then count them
instead of printing them. Neither command was written with the
other in mind. That is the whole idea. Small tools that do one
thing, joined end to end, and the joining is yours.

You can chain more than two:

    ls ship/crew | grep -v odysseus | wc -l

Every name on the roster, minus your own, counted.
