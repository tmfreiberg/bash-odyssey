Redirection -- > and >>

Every command prints its results to the screen. That is a
default, not a law. Put > and a filename after a command and the
results go into the file instead:

    cat > log.txt

Now cat is waiting for you to type, and what you type is going
into log.txt rather than onto the screen. Write your entry.
Press Ctrl-D on an empty line to finish.

Read it back:

    cat log.txt

Here is the part that will cost you something one day.

    >   sends output to a file, destroying whatever was in it
    >>  sends output to a file, adding to the end of it

They are one character apart and they do opposite things. > does
not warn you. > does not ask. If log.txt held nine days of
writing and you type

    cat > log.txt

then log.txt holds whatever you type next, and the nine days are
gone. There is no undo, no bin, and no copy unless you made one.

To add to a log without destroying it, always:

    cat >> log.txt

Get in the habit now, while the log is one day old.
