So it is already decided. It was decided in a house in an oak
wood by somebody who knew more than you.

Six men, or twenty men plus the hull.

You are not choosing here. Do not let this file pretend you are.
Circe answered it before you sailed, she was right, there is no
arrangement of oars or rope or cleverness that improves on her
answer. The whole of your reputation is for finding the third
option. There is no third option in this strait.

Two things you do in the next ten minutes are worth writing
down, because neither is to your credit.

The first: you tell the crew about Charybdis. You stand up in
the boat, you point out the whirlpool, you tell them to keep off
it, you give the steersman his line.

You do not mention Scylla.

You have six men who are going to die in about four minutes. You
know it. Not one of them knows it. Your reason is that they
would stop rowing, drop below the benches, hide -- which puts
the ship in the funnel, kills everyone aboard her. It is a good
reason. It is also the reason you have given yourself for every
single thing you have ever kept from them.

The second: you put your armour on.

She told you not to. She said it plainly: do not arm, it does
nothing, she is deathless. You put on the corselet anyway, you
take two spears, you go up to the foredeck to stand where you
can see the cliff, because you cannot make yourself do the other
thing, which is stand at the back with your hands empty while it
happens.

It accomplishes exactly nothing. She told you it would
accomplish exactly nothing.

Then you go into the narrows.
