The left-hand cliff goes up into cloud. It never clears. No man
could climb it barefoot or shod, not if he had twenty hands.

Halfway up there is a cave mouth facing west. Higher than a
bowshot. You could not put an arrow in it from the deck of this
ship on the calmest day of your life.

Scylla lives in it.

Twelve legs, all of them dangling, none of them touching
anything. Six necks, longer than any neck has business being,
each with a head on the end. Each head has three rows of teeth
set close, packed, full.

The body never leaves the cave. The necks do. They come out of
the mouth of it, all six, hanging down the rock face, the heads
working across the water below for dolphin, for seal, for
whatever the sea sends past. She has never gone hungry.

She barks. That is the sound. A voice no bigger than a puppy's,
coming out of a thing the gods themselves would not want to look
at.

Circe was blunt about her. Nobody has ever gone past that cave
whole. Do not stop to fight, she said, do not arm yourself, do
not slow the ship for one second: the thing is immortal, it
cannot be hurt, the only price of trying is more men.

Row hard. Take the loss. Keep going.
