Everyone on that ship is looking at Charybdis.

Every man. The steersman, because he has to be. The rowers,
because it is on their side, because it is making the noise,
because you told them about it yourself an hour ago. You, on the
foredeck, in armour, with two spears, staring at the whirlpool
you were warned about.

It goes down while you are watching. The sea empties out of the
strait, the sand at the bottom shows black, the whole funnel
roars.

Nobody is looking at the cliff.

She takes six out of the middle of the ship while your back is
turned.

You hear them over your head. That is how you find out: their
feet going up past your ear, then their voices from above,
calling your name, the last time any of them will use it.

You look up. Six men against the rock face, hands out, still
holding onto nothing, rising towards a cave mouth. They reach
it. They go in.

She eats them in the doorway where you can see it.

Homer says this: of everything he suffered searching out the
ways of the sea, that was the most pitiable thing his eyes ever
looked on.


She did not choose. You did not choose. She reached in, took
what her necks reached, they happened to be the six men nearest
the rail on the wrong side of a boat.

So this one is not yours to select.

Two men on that ship were out of her reach by accident of where
they were standing. You, on the foredeck in armour. Eurylochus,
at the steering oar in the stern, holding the line you gave him.
Everybody else was amidships at the oars, which is where the
necks came down.
