The other cliff is low enough to shoot across. A wild fig grows
on it, in full leaf, roots into bare rock.

Under the fig is Charybdis.

Three times a day she takes the water down. Not a current: the
sea goes into a hole. You can see the bottom of the strait when
it is out, black sand, rock, the sides of the funnel turning.
The noise is the swallowing you heard from a mile off.

Three times a day she sends it back up. It comes out boiling.
The spray goes over the fig tree, over the top of the cliff, out
of sight.

Any ship inside the funnel when it goes down does not come out.
Not damaged. Not driven ashore somewhere. Gone, with everybody
who was aboard her, in the time it takes to say so.

Circe's instruction on this one was a single sentence, delivered
without any of the qualification she gave to the rest.

Do not be there when it opens.

Keep hard against the other cliff. Row past Scylla's rock as
fast as men can pull.
