Two of them get up off the benches, come over, put more rope on

That is what they were told to do. Somebody nodded at somebody
else, both of them deaf, both of them watching their captain
scream silently against a mast with his face working. Then they
tightened it.

The singing thins out. Then it is behind you. Then there is
nothing but the oars.

When the island is well astern they take the wax out of their
own ears first. Then they untie you.

Nobody asks you what you heard.

That is the thing to notice. Twenty men row past the most famous
sound in the world with wax in their heads, they watch their
captain lose his mind against a rope for an hour, then they
unpick the knots, coil the line, sit down. Not one of them asks.

They have stopped asking. That happened somewhere between a
harbour with a narrow mouth, a house in an oak wood.
