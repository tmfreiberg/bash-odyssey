They see the ship. They start.

    less sirens/song.txt

You cannot read that. It is six hundred lines. It does not stop,
it does not pause, it has no shape you can hold, most of it is
the same three words over the top of a catalogue of everything
that ever happened on the plain at Troy.

That is the trick. It is not beautiful. It is not seductive in
any way a man could describe afterwards. It is a wall of
knowing, poured at you at a rate no one could take in, so that
the only way to have any of it is to stop the ship.

You are tied to a mast. You cannot turn pages. All you can do is
search.

    less scrolls/regex.txt

How much of it there is:

    wc -l sirens/song.txt

Now strip the chant. Nine lines in ten begin with the same two
words; a caret means "at the start of the line"; -v gives you
everything that does not match:

    grep -v "^We know" sirens/song.txt

That is what is left when the catalogue comes off. Twenty lines.
Every one of them is addressed to you by name or as good as.

Count how often they use it:

    grep -c Odysseus sirens/song.txt

Then find where in the song a thing sits, because position tells
you something about how they work:

    grep -n Odysseus sirens/song.txt

Now: three of the worst lines in that file are not in your
twenty. They start with the same two words as the chant, so your
filter threw them out with the noise. That is what filters do.
They are camouflaged deliberately. Go looking for your own
history instead:

    grep Ismarus sirens/song.txt
    grep "name you gave" sirens/song.txt
    grep "how many you have now" sirens/song.txt

There are two more lines you have been avoiding since you
started. You know what the words are. The bar means "or", -E
turns it on:

    grep -E "Penelope|Telemachus" sirens/song.txt

Nobody is making you run that one. You are tied to a mast in the
middle of a flat sea with your own crew ten feet away unable to
hear you. There is no version of this where you act on what
comes back.

    less sirens/meadow.txt
