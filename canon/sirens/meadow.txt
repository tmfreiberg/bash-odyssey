Look at the island while you are shouting.

It is a meadow. Flowers to the waterline, no trees, no house, no
smoke, nothing built. Two figures sitting in the grass.

Then you see what the grass is growing out of.

Bones in a heap. Not a battlefield spread, not scattered: heaped,
the way you would heap anything you had finished with, in one
place, over years. Men who rowed in to hear the rest of it.

Skin still on some of them, drying in the sun, going the colour
of old leather.

They did not fight. There is no weapon anywhere on that beach,
no broken timber, nothing burned. Every one of those men came
ashore of his own free will because he had been offered
something he could not put down.

You are shouting at your crew to turn the ship.

You are making faces at them. You are throwing your whole weight
sideways against the ropes. You have promised them things in the
last minute that you do not own.

They cannot hear you.
