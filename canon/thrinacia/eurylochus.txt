He waits until you are inland.

That is the detail worth holding onto: he does not make this
speech in front of you. He makes it while you are up the island
looking for a place out of the wind to pray, which is what you
do now, since praying is the only thing left that costs nothing.

Here is what he says to them. It is a good speech. It is the
best speech anybody makes in this whole voyage.

Every death is hateful to us, but the worst of them is hunger.
So: take the best of the cattle. Sacrifice to the gods properly,
every rite, nothing skimped. If we get home to Ithaca we build
Helios a temple, a rich one, full of offerings. If he is angry
enough about his cattle to sink us, then let him sink us -- I
would rather go all at once with my mouth open in the sea than
starve to death by inches on a beach.

Nobody argues with him. Not one man.

Read it again. Every part is reasonable. The sacrifice is
correct. The temple is a real promise from men who mean it. The
choice he sets out -- drowning fast against starving slowly --
is a genuine choice, honestly stated, which is more than you
gave them at the strait a fortnight ago.

He is your second in command. He has been right about as often
as you have. He was right about the cave. He was right about
Circe's house. He was right about landing here.

He is wrong once, at the end, about the only thing that was
ever going to matter.
