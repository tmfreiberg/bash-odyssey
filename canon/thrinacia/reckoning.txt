Now find out what it cost, exactly, if you can.

    ls thrinacia/herd | wc -l

That is what is left. Whether you know what it was before
depends on one command you were offered on the first evening,
before the wind turned, when there was nothing obviously at
stake.


IF YOU MADE THE COPY --

Then there is a second meadow on this island that nothing has
been taken out of. Set it against the real one:

    diff -r thrinacia/herd_as_found thrinacia/herd

diff compares two files. With -r it compares two whole
directories, walking down through both, reporting everything
that is in one but not the other.

Every line it prints beginning "Only in" is an animal that was
standing in that field when you walked up there with the sun
going down, is not standing in it now. Count them:

    diff -r thrinacia/herd_as_found thrinacia/herd | grep -c "^Only in"

You have a number. You will say it out loud in Alcinous' hall in
a few months, then again in your own hall in a few years, then
to anybody who asks for the rest of your life.

Remember this command. There is a version of it waiting at the
very end of this voyage, pointed at something larger than a
meadow.


IF YOU DID NOT MAKE THE COPY --

Then you have a field with some cattle in it. You know it is
fewer than there were. How many fewer is not recoverable, since
the only record of the before was a copy you did not make.

Nothing is recoverable. The cattle are eaten either way; the
difference is entirely in what you can say afterwards.

That is what the scroll on copying was about. It said so, in the
first week, in a sentence you read on a beach in Thrace: a copy
made beforehand is the only way back.


Six days of this. On the seventh the wind drops.
