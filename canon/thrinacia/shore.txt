You can hear the island before you raise it. Cattle lowing in
meadows, sheep behind them, the noise of a large farm working
quietly at a distance.

Thrinacia, which belongs to Helios, who is the sun.

Seven herds of cattle, fifty in each. Seven flocks of sheep, the
same. Nothing is born on this island. Nothing dies on it. The
number has been the number since before there were men to count
it. Two daughters of the sun keep them: one with a staff, one
with a crook, neither of whom has anything else to do.

You have been warned about this place twice.

A dead prophet on a black beach at the edge of the world told
you the order of it: leave the cattle alone, you may all reach
Ithaca; touch them, the ship goes, the crew goes, you personally
survive to arrive alone on somebody else's boat.

Circe told you the same in her own house, in her own words, more
carefully.

So you tell the crew to row past.

Eurylochus stands up. He says: we have been at the oars all
night, we are worn out, the wind gets up after dark, nobody
sails a strange coast in the dark on no sleep. One night ashore.
We eat what we carry. We go at first light.

Every man aboard is looking at you. Every man aboard agrees with
him.

He is not wrong about any of it. That is the trouble with him.
He has never once been wrong about the small thing.
