You are inland, out of the wind, praying.

You ask them for a way off this island. Every one of them, by
name, in order, properly.

What you get in return is sleep.

Not tiredness: sleep, poured over your eyelids by somebody, on a
hillside, in the middle of the day, in the middle of a sentence.
Homer says it plainly, without apology. They put you out.

The gods wanted this to happen. They wanted you asleep for it,
they wanted you blameless for it, they wanted a man to survive
who could report exactly what occurred with his own hands clean.

You will hold onto that afterwards. It is true. It also means
that every man you sailed with was steered onto a beach, starved
for thirty days, then left with one door open while the only
person who might have shut it was arranged into unconsciousness
a mile away.
