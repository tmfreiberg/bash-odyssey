You smell it before you are properly awake. Roasting fat, on the
wind, coming up from the shore.

You come down the hill knowing exactly what has happened,
knowing exactly how long it has been going on, working out with
every step how many of them it took.

Six days. They have been at it six days.

The best of the herd. Sacrificed correctly, every rite observed,
water sprinkled instead of the barley they no longer have, oak
leaves in place of the grain. Thigh bones wrapped, burned,
covered in fat. They did it properly. That is the part that
makes it worse: they were not thieves about it, they were
careful, they meant the temple.

Take them off the island. Nothing that has been eaten is still
standing in that meadow:

    ls thrinacia/herd | wc -l

Then remove what they took. Not all of it -- they are hungry men
working with bent hooks, not an army. Fifty head, over six days,
which is a herd.

The notice on those files says they may not be written to. It
does not say they cannot be removed. Your crew found this out
before you did:

    ls thrinacia/herd | head -50

That is the list. Remove exactly those.

    less scrolls/rm_recursive.txt

You can do it one at a time. You can do it with a pattern.
Either way rm will stop and ask, file by file, whether you mean
to remove something that is write-protected. That question is
the notice you set, read back to you.

Answer yes. Fifty times, if it comes to that. The notice asks; it
does not refuse. Your crew answered yes to every one of them, and
there was nobody standing over them to say otherwise.


Then Helios notices.

He goes to Zeus the same hour. He says: make them pay for my
cattle, or I stop shining for the gods altogether. I will go
down among the dead. I will shine there instead.

Zeus tells him to keep shining. Zeus says he will see to it
personally.

Meanwhile the gods put a sign on that beach, so that nobody
could later claim not to have been told.

The hides move. Flayed, salted, pegged out on the sand, they
crawl along the ground by themselves.

The meat bellows. On the spits, over the fire, raw pieces as
well as cooked, all of it making the noise a cow makes, going on
for as long as the fire is lit.

The animals are dead. That is the horror of it -- this is not
something struggling, it is butchered meat being made to speak.
A sign, delivered plainly, at the top of its voice, for six
days.

Your men go on eating.

Not out of defiance. Not because they doubt what they are
hearing. They sit around a fire listening to their dinner low at
them, then they eat it, because the alternative is another
morning on the rocks with a bent hook.

That is how hungry thirty days had made them.

    less thrinacia/reckoning.txt
