In the morning the wind is in the south.

You cannot sail into it off this coast. Not in a ship with one
sail, not out of a cove facing the way this one faces. You wait
a day. The wind holds. You wait another.

It holds for a month.

Thirty days of south wind on an island you were told to row
past, with the two herdswomen on the hill watching you not
leave.

The bread goes first. Circe put a good deal aboard; a good deal
is not thirty days for a crew. The wine lasts longer, which
helps in the way wine helps, which is to say it does not.

Then they are hunting.

Fish hooks bent out of anything that will bend. Birds taken off
the rocks with nets. Men going out at dawn along the shoreline,
coming back at dark with almost nothing, going out again the
next dawn.

Hunger is not dramatic. That is the thing nobody puts in the
songs. It is thirty days of men getting slower, quieter, more
careful about standing up quickly, more inclined to sit in the
same place looking at the same water.

Meanwhile there are three hundred fifty cattle in the meadow
above the cove. Fat. Slow. Unguarded except by two women with no
weapons. Not aging, not going anywhere, not needed by anyone for
anything.

Your men walk past them twice a day carrying nets.
