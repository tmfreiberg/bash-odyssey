The wind drops on the seventh day. You put to sea within the
hour. Nobody suggests waiting.

The island goes under the horizon behind you. Then there is
nothing in any direction but water.

Then one cloud.

It stands over the ship. Not near it: over it, holding position,
while the sea underneath goes black. The west wind arrives all
at once, screaming, out of nowhere, snapping both forestays
backwards so the mast goes down aft across the steersman's
skull. He goes over the side like a diver.

Then Zeus does it himself.

Thunderbolt into the hull. The whole ship fills with sulphur.
Every man aboard is in the water in the same second, carried
past the black hull on the swell like a flock of sea birds, no
shouting, nothing, gone.

The god takes their homecoming from them. That is the phrase.
Not their lives: their homecoming. They had one until six days
ago.

Take them off the roster. All of them. Every name in ship/crew
except your own:

    ls ship/crew

    less scrolls/rm.txt

Everyone. Eurylochus, who was right about the small things four
times, wrong once, at the end. Anybody you carried down from a
grove full of flowers eighteen months ago so that he could
drown here instead. Whoever is left of the men you counted on
the deck at Telepylus.

The ship is timber on the water.

Everything in the hold goes down with her. Whatever you carried
out of a burning town in Thrace in the first week, whatever you
have added since, all of it is on the bottom in eighty fathoms
inside a minute. There is no salvage from a hull that has been
hit by a thunderbolt.

    ls ship/hold
    rm -r ship/hold

One thing comes out of the water with you, because it was in
your hand when the mast came down, because you have carried it
since a beach at Ismarus with six dead men on it:

    less ship/log.txt

That is the whole of what you own now.

You lash the keel to the mast with the backstay, you get onto
that, the south wind comes up again to take you back towards the
strait you came through.

All night. Towards Charybdis.

You go over her at dawn as she takes the water down. The keel
goes into the funnel. You catch the fig tree above it, hang
there like a bat, no footing, nothing to climb, holding on for
the hours it takes her to bring the timbers back up.

Then you drop onto them, paddle clear with your hands.

Nine days on the open sea, alone, on the wreckage of a ship that
had a crew on it a week ago.

Write it down. This is the last entry you will make for a very
long time:

    cat >> ship/log.txt

On the tenth night the gods put you ashore on an island with one
person living on it.

    less ogygia/shore.txt
