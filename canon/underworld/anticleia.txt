Then your mother comes up out of the crowd.

Anticleia, daughter of Autolycus. She was alive when you sailed
for Troy. You have had twenty years of assuming she is at home,
because there was no reason to assume anything else, because
nobody sends word to a war.

She comes to the edge of the trench. She looks at you.

You do not let her drink.

That is the sentence. Circe said not one of them before
Tiresias, so you stand there with a sword between your own
mother and the blood, watching her not know you, because a shade
that has not drunk cannot speak, cannot recognise, cannot do
anything but want.

She waits at the edge with the rest of them.

You go on holding them off. The prophet is coming up the shore
with a gold staff, blind as he was alive, the only one down here
whose mind was left whole:
