You let her drink.

She knows you at once. She says: child, how did you get down
here alive? This is no place a living man walks into.

Then you ask her the questions, in the order a man asks them.

How did you die. Is my father alive. Is my son alive. Is my wife
still my wife, or has somebody in Ithaca had the sense to marry
her.

Her answers, one at a time.

Your father is alive. He does not come into the town any more.
He sleeps in the winter with the farm slaves by the fire in the
ashes, in old clothes, in summer out in the vineyard on the
ground on fallen leaves. He is waiting the way an old man waits.

Your wife is still in your house. She has not remarried. She is
holding on. She cries at night, every night, in the same room.

Your son holds his land. Nobody has taken it off him yet.

Nobody killed her. No sickness took her. She says it plainly:
she died of missing you. Longing for you, for your judgement,
for your kindness, took the life out of her.

Then you try to hold her.

Three times. You put your arms out, you close them, there is
nothing in them. Three times, because you are the man who does
not accept the first result, or the second.

She explains it without any bitterness, which is worse. This is
what the dead are. The sinews no longer hold the bone to the
flesh. Fire takes all that. What is left flies off like a dream.

Then she tells you to hurry back to the light, to remember all
of this, to tell your wife.

She stays where she is when you go.
