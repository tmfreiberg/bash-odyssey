IF YOU LEFT ELPENOR IN THAT CLEARING --

He is the first shade to reach the trench. Before Tiresias.
Before your mother. He got here ahead of your ship, because a
dead man travels light.

He speaks to you without drinking anything.

That is not allowed. It is the rule of this beach that a shade
who has not drunk cannot speak, cannot know you, can do nothing
but want -- your mother is standing four feet away proving it.

The rule does not hold him because he is not properly here. Nobody
burned him. Nobody heaped earth over him. Nothing was said. He is
still on a roof on Circe's island as far as the accounts of this
place are concerned, so the door was never shut behind him, so he
can still talk to the living.

He is not being spared. He is being left outside.

He is not angry. That is the difficult part. He is pleased to
see you, in exactly the way he was pleased about everything.

Then he asks you why he is not buried.

He was lying on his back on a roof on an island where the wine
was free, he got up too quickly in the dark, now he is here
without a grave marker, unwept, unburned. He says the word the
dead use for that. It means "not yet arrived".

He asks for three things. Burn him with his armour. Heap a mound
over him on the shore. Plant the oar he pulled at the top of it,
so that men who come after know somebody was there.

Then he says: swear it now, while you are standing where I can
hear you.

IF ELPENOR IS SITTING IN A GROVE ON ANOTHER ISLAND --

Then he is not here. He is one of three men you left under the
trees with flowers in their hands, happy, alive, no use to
anybody including himself.

Nobody meets you at this trench asking to be buried. There is no
promise to make. There is no detour to sail.

You will notice, some time later, that you got off lightly for a
decision that felt at the time like abandoning him.
