You are looking for a homecoming that tastes of honey.
A god will make it bitter.
Poseidon has not forgotten what you did to his son.
He will not kill you. He will make it long.

You may still get home, all of you, if you hold yourselves back
at one place.

Thrinacia. The island of Helios, who is the sun.
Cattle grazing on it, sheep grazing on it, every one of them his.
Helios sees everything. Helios hears everything.
That is what the sun is for.

Leave them alone. Touch nothing. Sail past.
Do that, hard as it will be, you may all reach Ithaca.

Harm them, I promise you this now, in order:
the ship is destroyed,
the crew are destroyed,
you personally survive,
you arrive late, on somebody else's boat, alone,
you find your house full of men eating your food
courting your wife
spending your son's inheritance in front of him.

You will kill them. All of them. In your own hall.
By trick or in the open, that part is yours to choose.

Then take an oar on your shoulder. Walk inland.
Keep walking until you meet a people who know nothing of the sea,
who eat their food unsalted,
who have never seen a ship painted at the bow.
You will know you have arrived when a stranger on the road
asks why you are carrying a winnowing fan.
Plant the oar there. Make sacrifice to Poseidon.
Then go home.

Your own death will come to you off the sea.
It will be gentle.
It will arrive when you are old, worn out, comfortable,
with your people prosperous around you.

That is all of it. It is a true account.
