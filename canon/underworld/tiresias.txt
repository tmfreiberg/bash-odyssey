He knows you before he drinks.

He is the other exception, for the opposite reason to the boy.
Elpenor keeps his voice because nothing here has claimed him yet.
Tiresias keeps his because Persephone let him keep it -- she took
the rest of him, left the mind alone, so that there would be one
in this place who could still think. That is why Circe sent you
down here for him rather than for anybody else.

He asks why you have left the light to come down among the dead,
which is a fair question, then tells you to stand back from the
trench so he can drink.

You let him. The sword comes down.

When he has drunk he speaks, straight through, without stopping.
It is long. He gives it once.

That is what he said. It is more than you can hold in your head
on a beach in the dark, so take an end of it at a time:

The beginning is about the sea. The end is about your own death,
which he describes without being asked, in detail, as a kindness.

The middle is about the cattle. Read it if you want it. Every
man who has ever been told a prophecy has read the middle.

Then he tells you that any shade you allow to drink will speak
to you, that any you keep back will stay silent. Then he is
gone, back into the house of Hades, unhurried.

Your mother is still standing at the edge of the trench.
