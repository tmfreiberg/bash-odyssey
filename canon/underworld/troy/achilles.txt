Achilles, son of Peleus. The best of them. Everyone always said
so, including the people who hated him.

He drinks. He knows you. He asks what in the world you are doing
down here among the used-up dead.

Then you say the thing you have been rehearsing. That he was
honoured like a god when he was alive, that he rules the dead
now, that he should not be sorry to be dead.

He stops you.

Do not gloss over death to me, he says. Do not do that.

He says he would rather be a hired hand on the land of a poor
farmer with nothing to his name, alive, working for wages,
than king over all these dead.

That is the answer. From him. The one who chose a short life
with glory over a long life at home, who knew exactly what he
was buying when he bought it, who is standing here on a black
beach telling you he got it wrong.

Then he changes the subject, immediately, to his son. Is he
fighting. Is he any good. Is he first in the line.

You tell him the truth: Neoptolemus is first in, last out,
untouched, extraordinary.

Achilles walks off across the field of asphodel taking enormous
strides, delighted, because his boy is a good soldier.
