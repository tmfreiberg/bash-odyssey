Agamemnon, son of Atreus, who commanded every ship at Troy.

He drinks. He knows you. He starts crying, reaches out to hold
you, cannot: there is no strength in him at all now, nothing of
the arms that ran that war.

You ask how he died. You are expecting a storm, or a last stand
somewhere on a beach.

He came home. That is the answer. He got the homecoming you have
been rowing towards for ten years, he walked up from his own
harbour into his own house, his wife had arranged a dinner.

Aegisthus killed him over the table. Cut him down at the meal
like an ox at a manger. His men were killed around him on the
floor among the wine cups. Clytemnestra, his wife, killed
Cassandra beside him while he was dying, close enough that he
heard it.

He says he tried to lift a hand as he went down. He could not.

Then he gives you the only advice he has, which he has clearly
been holding for a long time:

Do not sail openly into your own harbour. Do not announce
yourself. Come in quietly, find out what is in your house before
your house finds out what is in the bay.

Then, more carefully: your wife is not mine. Penelope is
sensible. She will not do this to you.

Then, after a pause, because he cannot help it:

All the same. Do not tell her everything. Tell her some of it.
Keep one thing back.
