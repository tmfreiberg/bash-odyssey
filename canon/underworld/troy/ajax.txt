Ajax, son of Telamon. The biggest man in the army. Second best
fighter at Troy after Achilles, which everybody knew, which was
the whole trouble.

When Achilles died they put his armour up as a prize. Two men
claimed it. The judges gave it to you.

He went to his tent, waited out the night, killed himself with
his own sword in the morning.

He is standing apart from the others now, out at the edge of the
crowd, in shadow.

You call him over. You say his name. You say the thing that is
true: that the army lost more when it lost him than when it lost
anyone except Achilles, that you would give the armour back if
giving it back were a thing that could be done, that no living
man wanted this.

You ask him to come and speak to you. You ask twice.

He does not answer you.

He turns around, walks into the dark with the other shades, does
not look back once. He has drunk nothing. He will not.

That is the end of it. There is no version of this where you fix
it. He was the one man at Troy who could not be talked round
alive; he is the one shade in the underworld who will not be
talked round dead.

You stand there a while.
